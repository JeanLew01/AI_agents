## Technical question answered from the package only

**Question.** In data-augmented MBD, how is the weight of a sample `Y^(0)` defined, and why is a max used rather than the product of the model and demonstration terms? What noise schedule and how many diffusion steps are used in the experiments?

**Answer from the package** (sections "A.4 MBD with Demonstration Explaination" and "A.5.2 MBD Hyperparameters"). The weight is `w(Y^(0)) = max{ w_model(Y^(0)) = p_d(Y^(0)) p_J(Y^(0)) p_g(Y^(0)), w_demo(Y^(0)) = p_demo(Y^(0)) p_J(Y_demo) p_g(Y_demo) }` (Eq. (32)); it enters the weighted mean `Ȳ^(0)` of Eq. (31) and the score of Eq. (30). With the product `w = w_demo w_model` (the plain posterior `p(Y^(0)|Y_demo) ∝ p_0(Y^(0)) p_demo(Y^(0))`), samples are drawn to both the model and the demonstration, so a suboptimal demonstration yields a compromise solution. With the max, `w_demo` dominates early, while `p_J(Y^(0))` is low, and pulls samples to the demonstration; once `p_J(Y^(0)) > p_J(Y_demo)`, `w_model` dominates and the sample converges to the optimum. The schedule is linear from `β_0 = 1 × 10⁻⁴` to `β_N = 1 × 10⁻²` with 100 diffusion steps for all tasks and `α_i = 1 − β_i`.
