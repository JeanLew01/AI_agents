## Technical question answered from the package only

**Question.** In the vision-based navigation experiments, how does the planner choose a goal when the goal lies in unobserved space, how is the obstacle constraint evaluated, and how did the hardware trials turn out?

**Answer from the package** (`paper.md`, sections 7.2, 7.3, 7.5 and 7.6, lines 396-463).
- Goal selection (7.2, Algorithm 1): while the RRT grows, a list f of frontier nodes is kept (nodes in unknown space connected to nodes in known space). A node counts as known if the NanoMap query reports that the query point is in free space. If the goal is not in the tree, or lies in an unknown region, the frontier node closest to the goal replaces it (`x_goal <- x_f <- x_goal.FIND_CLOSEST(f)`). Each iteration the RRT is initialised with the previous path, truncated at the start to the vehicle state and at the end to avoid new obstacles.
- Constraint (7.3): d(x) >= r, where d(x) is the mean distance to the K = 10 nearest depth points a_i, d(x) = (1/K) sum ||x - a_i||, with gradient (1/K) sum (x - a_i)/||x - a_i||. It is used with noise compensation by inflation; the experiments use a 3 meter radius, T_H = 1 s and 5 Hz replanning (7.5), and depth images downsampled by a factor of 10 (7.6).
- Outcome (7.6): four of six hardware trials rounded the building; in one of those four the aircraft later hit a light pole that the depth sensor saw too late. Two trials crashed into the building shortly after a corner because the camera did not observe the wall beyond the corner and new RRT paths were generated through the apparent gap. Cruise speed was 20 mph on straights, with post-stall manoeuvres in sharp turns.
